01 — The project

Closing the gap before the download

Embibe is a learning platform used by thousands of students. The start-up runs a website and a mobile app, and is in talks to ally with educational training centers. It collects data from students and charges only for advanced analysis and personalized learning recommendations — students can actually improve their test scores by fixing basic mistakes using its AI platform.

Fantasy partnered with Embibe to create a unique onboarding experience aimed at getting students to sign up for the app — because the biggest drop-off happened right between learning about the app and downloading it.

02 — My role

Leading the UI

As UI Lead, I owned the interface design of the onboarding — the visual style, the UI screens, and the way motion, 3D, and iconography came together into one coherent, welcoming experience — working alongside the UX lead and a broader craft team.

03 — The approach

Designing across two cultures

Designing for a market that isn't your own is never easy. Working with rich Indian color palettes and mixing them with a Northern-European sense of visual restraint was a beautiful challenge.

Two moves anchored it. First, a character — Embi — to guide the user through the experience (when we started, it was the one strong visual asset we had). Second, a new visual language we called Playful Tech: using the brand's existing elements, we built sharper, more expandable pieces that could grow across the whole product.

04 — The onboarding

Getting to the download

The onboarding borrows a familiar framework — Instagram Stories — with four or five stories that introduce the app's features fast. Based on Embi, we made five 3D icons, one for each of the app's main actions.

To explain the payoff, we built a Rube Goldberg animation where each thing triggers the next: show the variety of content → show progress → the stairs become graphs → your grades get better → take control of your learning.

Beyond onboarding, we designed a full set of topic icons and a search-and-filter across thousands of exams — and imagined how the whole visual language could expand across the brand.
